Sour Service Forgings: What NACE MR0175 Changes for 4130 and 30CrMo

An oil and gas manifold forging ordered to the same drawing behaves differently depending on whether the service is sweet or sour. Hydrogen sulphide changes the failure mechanism from overload to cracking at stresses well below yield. That is why NACE MR0175 exists, and why it constrains hardness, chemistry and heat treatment rather than just test results.

What sour service means

Sour service means the process stream contains hydrogen sulphide. In wet H2S, atomic hydrogen can enter the steel and cause sulphide stress cracking (SSC) or hydrogen-induced cracking (HIC). The failure can occur at stresses far below the yield strength, sometimes within days, and it usually starts at a hard spot, a segregated band or an inclusion stringer rather than at a gross defect.

NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 is the standard that defines what materials and conditions are acceptable in these environments. It is a materials standard, not a design code: it constrains the material state, and the designer remains responsible for stresses.

What MR0175 actually constrains

Hardness

The best-known requirement: carbon and low-alloy steels for sour service are limited to 22 HRC maximum (approximately 250 HV). Hardness is used as a proxy because hard regions are more susceptible to cracking, and because hardness can be measured on the finished part. This has a direct consequence for 4130 and 42CrMo-type grades — they can meet 22 HRC comfortably, but only if the tempering treatment is controlled and the hardness is checked, not assumed.

Chemistry

Composition limits exist for elements that promote cracking or degrade cracking resistance. Sulphur and phosphorus are held low to limit inclusion stringers; nickel content is restricted in some material groups; and for carbon steels the carbon equivalent influences what hardness is achievable after heat treatment.

Heat treatment

Sour service parts are normally supplied quenched and tempered, with the tempering temperature high enough to reach the hardness limit. A part that reaches 22 HRC by rapid cooling rather than by tempering is not the same material as one that is properly tempered to that hardness, even though the number matches.

Documentation

The standard is verified through the certificate: chemistry, heat treatment record, hardness survey (including the location of readings), and — where required — specific HIC or SSC testing.

Where 4130 and 30CrMo stand

ItemTypical position for 4130 / 30CrMo
Hardness capabilityMeets 22 HRC limit when quenched and tempered correctly
Strength levelHigh — which is exactly why hardness control must be explicit
Common forgingsAdapters, square tees, square crosses, valve bodies
RiskUnauthorised substitution at higher strength, or hardness checked only at the surface

Both grades are widely used for sour service manifold components. The failure mode to guard against is not the grade, it is the process: a heat treated to 26 HRC and certified with a single surface reading will look acceptable on the paperwork and unacceptable in the standard.

What to write on the purchase order

  • Service condition: sour, with H2S partial pressure and the applicable MR0175 / ISO 15156 material group.
  • Hardness limit and inspection method, including the number and location of readings — surface, mid-wall, and at the bore for hollow parts.
  • Heat treatment: quenched and tempered, with the tempering temperature range stated on the record.
  • Chemistry limits for S, P, and any element restricted by the material group.
  • Supplementary testing where applicable: hardness survey, and HIC testing to NACE TM0284 if the application calls for it.
  • Ultrasonic inspection to a named standard and class — internal soundness and sour service are separate requirements, and both are needed.
  • Marking and traceability: heat number, and where required the MR0175 marking.

Two mistakes that recur

First: treating MR0175 as a certificate checkbox. A supplier who writes MR0175 compliant on the certificate without a hardness survey and heat treatment record has not demonstrated compliance, only asserted it.

Second: checking hardness at the wrong place. Surface readings on a quenched and tempered forging can miss a harder sub-surface zone. For thick parts, readings should be taken through the section or at agreed locations, and stated in the report.

Frequently asked

Is 4130 suitable for sour service?

Yes, provided it is quenched and tempered to meet the hardness limit required by NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 — typically 22 HRC maximum for carbon and low-alloy steels — and that hardness is actually verified at agreed locations. The grade is not the issue; the delivered condition is.

What hardness limit applies in sour service?

For carbon and low-alloy steels the widely applied limit in MR0175 / ISO 15156 is 22 HRC maximum. Some material groups and conditions carry additional restrictions, so the applicable requirement should be taken from the standard for your specific environment and confirmed on the order.

Do I need HIC testing as well as UT?

They answer different questions. Ultrasonic testing finds volumetric defects; HIC testing evaluates the steel's resistance to hydrogen-induced cracking in the specified environment. Whether HIC testing is required depends on the application and should be stated in the purchase order.
