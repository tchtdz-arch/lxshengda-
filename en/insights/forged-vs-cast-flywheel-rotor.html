Forged vs Cast Flywheel Rotor: Why Fatigue Life Decides the Choice

A flywheel energy storage rotor spins continuously for years, at high tip speed, with stress cycling every revolution. The design driver is not static strength — it is high-cycle fatigue and the consequence of internal defects. That is why the rotor is forged, and why the fatigue test position matters as much as the test result.

The duty cycle is the specification

A rotor in a flywheel energy storage system accumulates cycles fast. At a few thousand revolutions per minute, one hour of operation is already a few hundred thousand stress cycles; a year is in the billions of load reversals at the stress-concentration features — the bore, the stepped shaft transition, keyways and fits. Under those conditions the rotor does not fail by yielding. It fails, if it fails, by fatigue initiating at an internal or surface defect.

That single sentence determines the manufacturing route: the material must be dense, homogeneous and free of the defect types that act as fatigue initiators.

What casting gives you, and what it costs you

Casting is attractive for shape freedom and cost. Its weakness is exactly where a rotor is most sensitive:

  • Shrinkage porosity in the thermal centre — which for a disc-type rotor is precisely the highest-stressed region.
  • Non-metallic inclusions and slag entrapment, acting as initiation sites.
  • Coarse, dendritic as-cast structure with segregation of alloying elements.
  • No grain flow to follow the stress pattern — the material is isotropic in the worst sense.

Cast rotors do exist, and for low-speed, low-energy applications they can be adequate. For high tip speed they are used rarely, and when they are, they tend to be heavily derated.

What forging does

Forging works on the same steel mechanically rather than thermally:

  • Closes internal porosity. Adequate reduction ratio welds shut internal voids, which is why forging ratio is a controlled parameter and not a casual one.
  • Refines grain size and breaks up carbides, raising both strength and toughness at the same time — a combination castings cannot offer.
  • Creates continuous metal flow lines that follow the part contour, improving fatigue resistance at fillets and transitions where cracks start.
  • Makes internal quality verifiable. A forged and heat-treated rotor can be fully inspected by ultrasonic testing after final heat treatment, which is not equally true for a casting.

Why the fatigue specimen position matters

Fatigue data quoted from a specimen taken near the surface says little about a rotor. The critical region is the centre of the section, where solidification effects and forging reduction are least favourable, and where the stress state is complex. A fatigue verification that is worth the paper it is printed on samples the centre, at the mid-length of the forging.

Our rotor forgings are fatigue tested at the centre position to 10 million cycles, alongside piece-by-piece ultrasonic inspection and full chemical and mechanical testing. That is the level of evidence a flywheel OEM needs before putting a rotor into a series product.

Buyer's checklist for a flywheel rotor forging

  1. Melting route — vacuum degassing at minimum; ESR or VAR for higher-duty rotors.
  2. Forging ratio and reduction sequence, not just "forged".
  3. Heat treatment record — full cycle with temperatures, holding times and cooling method, traceable to the heat number.
  4. UT after final heat treatment, to a named standard and acceptance level, piece by piece.
  5. Fatigue verification at the centre position, with the specimen location stated in the report.
  6. Mechanical properties at the specified sampling position — tensile, yield, elongation, reduction of area and impact energy.
  7. Traceability — heat number on the part, matching certificate, heat treatment record and test reports.

Frequently asked

Why not just cast the rotor and derate it?

Derating a casting for a high-tip-speed rotor offsets most of the cost advantage, because the mass and diameter have to grow to carry the same energy at lower stress — and the resulting inertia often does not fit the application envelope. Forging lets you use the material closer to its fatigue capability.

At what point in production is UT performed?

After final heat treatment, and again after rough machining where the customer requires it. Testing before final heat treatment is not accepted as evidence for the delivered part, because heat treatment can open internal defects.

Can you supply the rotor as an assembly with the shaft?

Yes. We supply rotor bodies, and rotor assemblies including the stepped shaft, machined to drawing. Both configurations ship with chemical, mechanical, UT and fatigue documentation.
