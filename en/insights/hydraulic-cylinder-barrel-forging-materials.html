Hydraulic Cylinder Barrel Forgings: 27SiMn, E355 or 42CrMo?

A cylinder barrel looks like a simple tube, but the material choice fixes the pressure rating, the welding procedure, the honing behaviour and the inspection plan. This note compares the three grades most often specified for large-diameter barrels, and explains when a forging beats a seamless tube.

The three common choices

GradeEquivalentsTypical use
27SiMnChinese GB grade; no direct DIN/JIS equivalentStandard hydraulic cylinder barrels in the Chinese market
E355EN E355 (1.0580)European drawings, welded and honed barrels
42CrMoDIN 1.7225 / JIS SCM440 / AISI 4140High-pressure barrels, rods, heavy-duty cylinders

What each one is good for

27SiMn — the domestic workhorse

27SiMn is a manganese-silicon steel developed for hydraulic supports and cylinder barrels. It offers good strength after quenching and tempering, reasonable machinability and low cost, and it is widely available in China. Its weakness is weldability: the relatively high carbon and manganese content makes it crack-sensitive in welding, so preheat and post-weld heat treatment are not optional. If a barrel is to be welded to a flange or a port block, that matters.

E355 — the European default

E355 is a structural steel specified for hollow sections and tubes, and it appears on European cylinder drawings because the barrel is often made from a honed tube rather than a forging. It welds well and machines predictably, but its strength as supplied is lower than a quenched and tempered 27SiMn or 42CrMo. For high pressure, the wall thickness has to grow — which then costs weight and material.

42CrMo — for pressure and duty cycle

42CrMo (AISI 4140 family) gives the highest strength of the three after quenching and tempering, with good toughness and fatigue resistance. It is the choice for high-pressure barrels, large bore cylinders and applications with pressure cycling. It costs more and needs proper heat treatment control, and like 27SiMn it requires care in welding.

Forged barrel or honed tube?

Both routes are legitimate, and the choice is usually made on diameter and wall thickness:

  • Seamless tube — economical for standard diameters, readily available in E355 and equivalent grades, and convenient when the barrel is short and the pressure moderate.
  • Forged barrel — better where the diameter is large, the wall is thick, or the barrel carries ports, flanges or a closed end. Forging gives a continuous grain flow around the section, closes internal porosity and allows UT of the full volume before machining.

For thick-wall, high-pressure barrels, a forged and quenched-and-tempered shell with a bored and honed bore is often the more reliable route than a tube, because the internal quality can be verified and the mechanical properties are uniform through the wall.

Points that decide whether the barrel works

  1. Wall thickness uniformity. An eccentric wall means uneven stress and a bore that cannot be honed true. This is a forging and machining discipline, not a material property.
  2. Heat treatment through the wall. A barrel that is hard on the outside and soft at the bore will not hold pressure as designed. Specify quench and temper with hardness checked at the bore, not only at the surface.
  3. UT before boring. Internal defects are cheapest to find before the bore is finished. Specify the standard and acceptance level — GB/T 6402, ASTM A388 or EN 10228-3.
  4. Weldability, if welding follows. If the barrel will be welded, say so at enquiry stage. It changes the material recommendation and the heat treatment sequence.
  5. Bore finish and tolerance. Honing, skiving and roller burnishing all have different starting requirements; the barrel supplier needs to know which follows.

What to write on the enquiry

  • Bore diameter, wall thickness and overall length, with the drawing.
  • Working pressure and whether it is static or cycling.
  • Material preference and the standard it must meet.
  • Whether the barrel will be welded, and to what.
  • UT standard and acceptance level; hardness check position.
  • Delivery condition: as-forged, rough machined, or bored and honed.

Frequently asked

Is 27SiMn equivalent to E355?

No. They are used for similar products but are different steels: 27SiMn is a Chinese grade with higher carbon and manganese, quenched and tempered for strength; E355 is a European structural steel supplied in the as-rolled or normalized condition. A drawing written for E355 will not be satisfied by 27SiMn without a review of strength, weldability and heat treatment.

Can you supply the barrel already honed?

We supply as-forged, rough machined or bored and honed barrels depending on the requirement. For honed supply, please state the bore tolerance, surface finish and the honing allowance expected, so the pre-honing bore can be prepared correctly.

Why is a forging preferred over a tube for thick walls?

Because the full volume can be inspected by UT before machining, the grain flow follows the section, and the mechanical properties can be made uniform through a thick wall by quenching and tempering. Thick-wall tubes are limited by what the tube mill can produce and by the inspection coverage available on the tube.
