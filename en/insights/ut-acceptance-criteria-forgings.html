Specifying Ultrasonic Testing for Forgings: GB/T 6402, ASTM A388, EN 10228-3 and JIS G 0587 Compared

A certificate that says 'UT: OK' tells you almost nothing. Ultrasonic testing of forgings is defined by a standard, a calibration reflector, a scanning coverage and an acceptance level — and those four things decide whether your forging is actually acceptable. This is how the main standards differ, and what to write on the order.

The four standards buyers meet most often

Forging suppliers in China quote UT against GB/T 6402. European drawings usually call for EN 10228-3 (ferritic and martensitic steel forgings). North American drawings call ASTM A388/A388M. Japanese drawings call JIS G 0587. All four use the pulse-echo method and all four will find laminations, inclusions, shrinkage and cracks. They are still not interchangeable, for reasons that are administrative as much as technical.

Two consequences follow. First, ASTM A388 has no built-in acceptance level — if your PO does not state one, the supplier can pass anything that produces a clean report. Second, GB/T 6402 and EN 10228-3 use different reference reflectors by default, so a report written to one is not automatically acceptable to an inspector holding the other. Additional testing or re-evaluation is usually required.

Five things a UT specification must state

  1. Standard and revision — "EN 10228-3:2016", not "EN 10228".
  2. Acceptance level — quality class 3, or a stated maximum equivalent reflector size at a stated depth.
  3. Coverage — 100 % of the specified volume, or the surfaces and directions required. For thick sections, angle-beam scanning for radial defects should be explicit.
  4. Test surface and timing — ultrasonics is normally performed after final heat treatment and after rough machining, when the surface finish allows good coupling. Testing an as-forged, scaled surface produces a report that means very little.
  5. Reporting — defect location, depth, equivalent size and a plot for any indication above the recording level; operator certification level (ISO 9712 / ASNT Level II or above).

Common failure modes in Chinese certificates

  • Test performed before final heat treatment. Heat treatment can open up internal defects, so the report no longer describes the delivered part.
  • Report quotes a standard but no acceptance class.
  • As-forged surface tested, with heavy coupling loss and no mention of it.
  • Only straight-beam scanning used on a thick section where radial defects are the main risk.
  • "Equivalent to ASTM A388" claimed without any statement of reference reflector — the two are not equivalent in that sense.

How we run UT

Every forging is inspected piece by piece — no sampling. The default standard is GB/T 6402, and ASTM A388/A388M, EN 10228-3 or JIS G 0587 are performed on request, with the acceptance level agreed in the order. Testing is carried out after final heat treatment; where required we test again after rough machining. Reports carry defect location and size, and third-party witnessing (SGS, BV, TUV) can be arranged.

Frequently asked

Is GB/T 6402 the same as ASTM A388?

No. Both are ultrasonic test-method standards for steel forgings and both use pulse-echo technique, but they differ in reference reflector type, default frequency range, minimum thickness and — most importantly — acceptance criteria. GB/T 6402 defines quality grades inside the standard; ASTM A388 leaves acceptance limits to the purchaser. Reports are generally mutually recognisable only after additional evaluation.

Do you test every forging or sample them?

Every forging is tested individually. For rotor and other safety-critical parts this is non-negotiable, and we apply the same practice to standard forgings unless the customer specifies sampling.

Can you arrange SGS / BV / TUV witnessing?

Yes. Third-party inspection can be arranged at the customer's cost, either witnessing our in-house testing or performing independent testing. Please state the requirement in the order so it can be scheduled with production.
